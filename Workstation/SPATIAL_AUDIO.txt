Spatial audio spheres

Tab switches between the arrangement and spatial view when the piano roll is
closed. Each observed audio source track has a glossy metallic sphere in the
spatial view's open background. The Swift reference supplies metalness 0.9,
roughness 0.2, a gray environment, white directional light and independently
moving theme-colored and cool-blue lights. Native analytic ellipsoid geometry
uses the existing shared OpenGL/GLES/Vulkan/WebGPU optical renderer. No hall,
walls, grid, extra background plane or sphere shadows are added.

The camera remains the workstation's orthographic camera. Sphere size and
position create the perspective illusion; shallow geometric depth handles
overlap. There is one sphere per track, not a duplicate for every overlapping
clip. Stable track identity determines color. Stereo balance moves the source
left/right, correlation and channel-energy overlap determine its horizontal
stretch, and log-frequency spectral centroid determines height. The stereo
image is the track's source image before the L/S/FF/R routing fold and master
gain. It is not a visualization of downstream speaker or room processing.

Distance has two inputs: the source's pre-gain RMS and its effective fader gain.
Quiet passages recede even with the fader untouched. Lowering the fader also
moves the source back. Attack/release smoothing keeps the image readable.
The reference amplitude pulse scales the sphere with the current signal.
Silence has no invented spectrum or motion; quiet handles remain selectable.
Unobserved sources and MIDI without a connected synthesis path report their
unavailable measurement state without drawing placeholder balls. Playback
cessation ages out the observation.

Click and drag a sphere upward/back to lower its manual track gain, or
downward/near to raise it. The wheel provides a fine gain adjustment over a
sphere. Gain uses SetTrackMix and its normal finite 0..16 linear-gain bounds.
Stereo and frequency remain measurements; these gestures do not introduce a
pan control or an EQ. Gain updates use the document revision and refresh the
authoritative snapshot. Changing signal amplitude cannot change the pointer's
gain baseline during a grab. Active CC gain automation blocks manual sphere
gain changes and reports why. Escape, lost pointer/focus, removal or conflicting
document edits cancel capture. Arrangement, piano-roll and sphere gestures keep
separate ownership. Switching views never transfers an already held click.

The normal playback producer requests measurements during its existing
RenderRange calls, including synthesized and plugin-processed PCM. It retains
up to 128 small observation packets without PCM. EnvironmentAudioSystem.Status
selects the newest fully consumed block matching the current transport
generation, document revision and sample rate. AudioWorkstationSystem.Step
uses that selected packet instead of the producer's future observation.
The UI never re-renders a range or advances a stateful plugin for visualization.
A seek waits for new-generation audio; pause ages the last display toward silence.
This is source analysis, not hardware loopback. Block completion, UI scheduling
and hardware buffering limit synchronization; display is not sample-accurate.

All source ownership, FFT, JSON and System calls stay on the control thread.
The native callback is unchanged by this feature. Ordinary RenderRange calls
skip analysis; analyze_visualization:true requests measurement without publication and
publish_visualization:true also publishes it. The cached small
revision/sequence-tagged visualization is exposed by Snapshot; it retains no
PCM. The renderer receives validated bounded packets for up to 64 sources.

Validation

--check-audio-spheres BACKEND opens a finite native diagnostic using three real
generated PCM tracks with different levels, stereo images and frequencies.
It exercises the same document ingestion, range renderer, observation,
projection and shaders as playback. It does not start a device, populate normal
sessions or establish live-audio correctness. Source analysis, controller,
packet validation, native captures and live interaction are separate checks.
See output/checks/spatial-audio-* for recorded evidence when validation runs.
Phone and live hardware behavior require separate playtesting.
