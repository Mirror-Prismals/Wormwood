Worker

The actual player GLB supplies skin and limb lengths, with workshop clothing
colors. Feet stay planted, legs bend, the torso leans and the arms solve to the
accepted tool wrists. The material-space hand anchors, physical shafts,
finite-mass tools and OGC projection are shared with gameplay. The motor asks
for an edge-first insertion, levering, lifting, carrying and a tip around the
upper hand. Surface selection samples remaining matter at each new digging
stroke. No material is erased or attached to the shovel to produce a result.
The tree uses the same anchored base fibres and retained crown as game trees.

A reduced muscle actuator now also drives held tools in gameplay: activation
and relaxation, force/length and force/velocity factors, fatigue, target-velocity
tracking and finite hand force/wrist torque. The worker phases coordinate
activation with the motion. Its quasistatic body lean responds to accepted tool
reaction within a planted stance. This is a supported motor model, not a full
anatomical muscle simulation or dynamic full-body ragdoll. The workshop does
not run a separate copy of the game navigation/aim controller. It isolates the
shared tool/material path so reach and contact are visible.

Captions show current hand effort, actual current tool contact, accumulated
contact work and peak carried material samples. The detailed report includes
edge-to-target distance and grip error. A requested stroke is not a claim that
it succeeded; the physical result and counters must be judged in play.
The digging worker keeps its shovel when obstructed by rock; the older layered
workshop remains the shovel-to-pick tool-exchange example.

Winter materials

Snow uses the existing APIC material-point transport with its own compressive
plastic projection, limited tensile strain, permanent densification and
hardening. Its transport step accounts for the faster elastic response of
compacted snow; density follows each parcel's current phase. OGC handles
shovel/ground boundaries. Snow initially starts at -6 C;
after 20 physical seconds its demonstration warms the air to +8 C. Heating runs
at 180 times physical time, labelled in the caption. A melted snow parcel keeps
its mass and changes to the liquid constitutive law and corresponding volume.

Ice uses density 917 kg/m3 and brittle bonded convex cells with ice-specific
strength and toughness. The finite hydrostatic bath applies buoyancy using the
submerged volume of each accepted cell. Its water temperature cools as heat
passes into ice and it exchanges heat with ambient air. Liquid mass received
from melting joins the bath, including airborne meltwater that falls into it. In air,
melted ice becomes explicit water parcels with the source mass and velocity,
using the APIC/OGC liquid path. Both ice showcases use 240x thermal time, +10 C
air and initially -5 C ice; the bath starts at +12 C. The contact pick and
mechanics run at ordinary simulation speed.

Energy first warms frozen matter to zero, then pays latent heat before
conversion. Convection coefficients are authored approximations; immersed heat
exchange is faster than air exchange. Ice melts cell by cell at exposed faces;
this is a coarse thermal front, not continuous ablation or molecular structure.
The bath is a supported hydrostatic reservoir, not a resolved wave/fluid tank.
Refreezing, snow accumulation/precipitation, seasonal landscape snow coverage,
thermal stress and conduction inside an intact ice block are not implemented.

The game now samples an authored air temperature from Eastern calendar day,
time of day, elevation and the same weather-front phase used by clouds. Active
game water parcels have temperature/air exchange at normal thermal time.
This is game climate, not live real-world weather. Natural water generation has
not been replaced with a planet-wide thermal/fluid calculation, and the new
snow/ice deposits are currently workshop specimens rather than world coverage.

References used for the model

Stomakhin et al., A Material Point Method for Snow Simulation (2013): elastic
stretch projection and plastic-volume hardening. The implementation adapts
those ideas to this engine's existing transport and OGC contacts; it does not
claim to reproduce Disney's full solver.
