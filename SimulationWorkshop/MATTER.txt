Current source changes following build 2493.6.13

Fine sand uses sand.rs, an original bounded explicit MPM adaptation informed by
Klar et al. (2016). Quadratic B-spline transfers carry mass/momentum to a sparse
52 mm computational grid. APIC stores affine velocity. A symmetric elastic
strain tensor provides Hencky stress and a Drucker-Prager return map. Compression
supports shear up to the frictional yield cone; loose expansion has no tension.
Plastic dilation is recorded so loose material can close before elastic loading.
Two explicit internal predictions fit each 1/240-second contact substep; the
accepted OGC motion, rather than the unrestricted prediction, updates strain
and affine velocity. This particle-boundary coupling is our reduced adaptation,
not a reproduction of the paper's implicit grid-boundary solver. Resolution,
constitutive constants and coupling still need observational calibration.
Particles represent parcels of many fine grains. The display is a continuous
bulk skin, not a pile of visible computational octahedra. The original discrete
model remains unchanged under matter_pebbles. No physical grain is subdivided
or erased; this patch does not implement an entire beach or grain-level detail.

The new axe has a finite sharpened edge, broad splitting cheeks and a haft along
the head's eye. Its edge stays aimed into the specimen during the downward arc,
then holds and rocks the wedge through bounded drive forces. Along-grain aim
uses a real existing end-grain interface; it does not create or break that seam.
Crack-front neighbours are recorded from shared material-space edges, preserving
the path when open faces rotate. Work follows the strongest admissible front,
paying each interface's remaining energy before progressing. Growth-ring radial
direction modulates cleavage resistance. Transverse edge cuts retain the same
stronger fibre law. Crack visibility uses thin face ribbons rather than rods.
Full log separation is an intended outcome, not a result verified in this pass.

The Compare forces selector appends _compare to a single-material case. The
three 17-second trials each start with an identical fresh specimen and initial
tool pose. Actuator limits are 45, 240 and 800 N; motion targets, mass and material
parameters are identical across trials. Results retain measured force peaks,
head kinetic energy, contact work, damage, peak lifted material and bucket
containment. The total simulated clock remains monotonic across resets.
These limits control the motor, not the instantaneous impact force. No expected
fracture count is baked into a trial. Reset restarts the entire comparison.

Layered excavation uses layered.rs and shared substep helpers in coupling.rs.
An 85 mm rooted mat sits above roughly 250 mm of cohesive soil and a 146 mm rock
bed. The lower rock cells remain anchored. Root constraints carry equal and
opposite reactions between the mat and upper soil, with slack for settling and
irreversible tensile rupture. Each iteration solves all three materials against
one OGC contact response; tool contact work is attributed to the nearest physical
boundary rather than spent repeatedly in multiple materials. All mass persists.
The spade cuts/lifts turf, deposits to the side, and repeats soil scoops. Only a
measured clear patch permits the shovel/rock resistance and pick stages. A tool
exchange happens outside the specimen. No stage erases cover or resets material.
Five unsuccessful soil passes report remaining cover and retain the scene.
The section uses fixed soil resolution and prepared rock interfaces; it is not
yet installed in the planetary terrain. Its turf has two displayed blades per
crown. Mesh reconstruction reserves a per-material budget within 8192 triangles.
The iOS bridge now uses the engine's case registry, including the previously
omitted cross-grain and blunt-contact cases. All explicit graphics paths remain.

Implementation
Physics/matter/mod.rs owns this independent PhysicsSystem material path.
Turf is a dedicated path in turf.rs, not Kind::Dirt or its implicit skin. It
contains 96 irregular triangular-prism elements with elastic edge/shear and
signed volume constraints. Boundary tissue represents its attachment to the
surrounding lawn; interior lower nodes can lift. Separate cohesive soil seams
and tensile-only root bundles join adjacent elements. Four root bundles cross
each seam at different depths. Matrix cracks can open while surviving roots
still carry load. Root overload and sharp-edge contact work sever the bundles
permanently; broken ends are carried in their own element's coordinates.
The mass-bearing root/soil volume and its exposed faces are used by OGC and
rendering. Contact-neighbour exclusions allow coincident newborn crack faces
to open before OGC takes over; unilateral support prevents normal overlap
while opening. Conservative envelope checks cull turf self queries only when
elements cannot approach within the bounded next iteration's displacement.
Folded or nearby detached tissue still enables OGC self contact.
This is a reduced root-reinforced tissue model, not a dirt
particle pile. It uses a fixed material resolution and uncalibrated strengths.
Established foliage has 384 crowns, each with four curved tapered blades.
Those blades are attached geometry, without independent contact or biological
growth. Crown orientation follows the deforming mat. The old germination
demonstration remains explicitly selectable as matter_seedlings. Its growth
obligations are retained rather than asserted for the new established turf.

Wood, stone and rubble now use solid.rs and solid_geometry.rs. Closed game
trunk/rock volumes are partitioned into irregular weighted Voronoi cells with
matching shared faces. Every cell has integrated mass and inertia and accepts
only a rigid transformation, conserving piece shape and volume after fracture.
Wood's cross-section sites are aligned between axial stations to form continuous
fibre bundles. Independent 3D seed jitter was retired for wood because it made
the weak interfaces interlock like an isotropic rock tessellation.
The wood source is Vegetation::Tree and Geometry::build_seasonal, capped at the
ends of a 0.62 m trunk section; it is not an entire tree or a brown soil mound.
Its exterior uses bark shading and its interior uses material-space wood rings.

Rock's compliant interfaces carry tensile/shear load and accumulate fracture work.
Wood now uses stiff continuous fibre bundles with volume-integrated mass and
inertia, including parallel-axis contributions. The cross-fibre matrix uses
brittle cohesive interfaces, with critical opening 2*toughness/strength and
no damage-dependent stiffness reduction. Accepted excessive opening in a
loaded or already damaged interface releases
the remaining interface energy rather than producing a visible elastic tether.
A broken interface cannot apply a restoring force. The old damage-softened
wood network is explicitly retired. A whole-connected-component rigidity trial
was also rejected: it locked partially cracked wood until every connection
through an entire specimen had failed. Only continuous fibres are now grouped.
The detached log rests on OGC's table instead of pinning every basal column.
Longitudinal crack fronts can receive a share of local tool work
from an existing aligned crack; the work is divided, not copied along it.
The physical axe plane biases the fracture direction. Cross-grain interfaces require the cutting edge near the interface and the
higher cross-grain strength/toughness. An additional sideways stroke uses the
same specimen/law for the chop comparison. This rigid-fibre approximation
does not model intact wood's small elastic bending or arbitrary cut planes.
The transverse stroke aims at a prepared axial section and holds its cutting
plane during the strike. Reusing the splitting stroke's pitch arc raised the
edge above that section at impact and spent its energy peeling fibres instead.
Wood interfaces are weaker and less tough along fibres; rock has brittle,
isotropic interfaces. In rock, partial damage reduces opening stiffness and residual
strength while retaining compressive support. Local compressive tool contact supplies splitting work
only above a strength threshold. Pressure uses force over projected incident
triangle area clipped to the contact band around the material face. Counting
the whole wedge face incorrectly diluted a cutting edge's pressure. Work uses
the greater of force-distance work and the contact's share of lost incoming
head kinetic energy; a stopped strike can load a crack even with little travel.
Only already-present momentum counts. An evaluated free-prediction energy
alternative was retired because a stationary blocked actuator could otherwise
accumulate fictitious impact energy on every substep.
Kinetic energy uses lumped head mass, not a calibrated impact/contact-patch law.
These are mesh-dependent estimates, not a continuum
stress solution. Fracture exposes existing faces; it does not spawn chips or
delete matter. The same law handles the pick and the broad bucket comparison.
Local work is weighted by distance, fracture resistance and existing damage,
so it concentrates in weakened interfaces instead of becoming uniform fatigue.
Broken interfaces reaching the exterior draw narrow crack seams even when their
opening is subpixel. Seams depict actual broken connectivity, not timed decals.
Automatic strikes have 45, 240 and 480 N drive limits. goal() limits actuator
acceleration; contact projection no longer clamps accumulated momentum as
though it were additional hand force. These are uncalibrated material presets.

OGC queries the actual exposed solid faces, tool shell and table. Intact
component self pairs are excluded. Newly separated coincident faces require
a 3 mm opening before their exclusion retires and OGC contact takes over.
Before that opening, matching crack faces retain unilateral normal support:
breaking adhesion must not allow one solid cell to compress into another.
Partial cracks within an otherwise connected component have this local support,
but do not have general OGC self-collision after sliding or reopening.
These are explicit limitations: there is no crack-tip adaptive refinement,
continuous collision detection, calibrated FEM, or molecular simulation.

Dirt retains material.rs compliant bonds, tensile damage and compressive
plasticity, with overlapping shape-preserving clusters that follow plastic
rest lengths. Broken bonds leave those clusters. Pebbles use discrete fixed-size
grains, normal contact multipliers and static/dynamic Coulomb friction. The
inertial target stays fixed throughout all coupled iterations for particles,
solid cells and shoots; contact response is not reapplied as free acceleration.

For dirt/sand, OGC supplies tool/material and table force, Hessian and conservative
bounds. It queries closed octahedral material proxies plus shoot ribbons, and
the actual closed tool heads. Material self pressure/friction is the internal
particle solve. Particle motion reduces contact responses to a shared
translation; tool motion uses the existing finite-mass rigid OGC solve.
The implicit display skin is distinct from those contact proxies. Fine local
samples reduce that approximation near a tool; surface matching is not exact.

adapt.rs splits dirt samples locally before the approaching tool reaches
them. Each sample becomes eight smaller samples with unchanged total mass and
linear momentum. Existing bond area is partitioned, and strain, plastic rest
length and damage are carried forward. Child samples add internal structure.
The active material budget is 1,000 samples, with two refinement levels.
Refinement starts between 1.45 and 2.25 sample radii from the physical tool.
Gravity and bulk movement alone do not cause global refinement. This narrows
the former eager activation region; it does not reduce the maximum local levels.

Settled compact sibling groups may coarsen away from contact only in the
interior. External surviving connections are combined without recreating
failed bonds. Mass, linear momentum and stored angular momentum transfer to
the parent. Damaged interfaces veto merging. Sand has no permanent bond
network; frictional load-bearing contacts arise from current proximity. This
is conservative sibling coarsening, not unrestricted adaptive remeshing.

skin.rs reconstructs dirt's sparse implicit skin with marching tetrahedra and
analytic field normals. The sampling grid exists only during meshing; it is
not the material representation. Disconnected cohesive groups are evaluated
separately, so a nearby detached piece does not gain a bond or become part of
its neighbour. Reconstruction adapts its output spacing to the existing
visual triangle budget, while retaining the material particles themselves.
Pebbles use separate octahedral grain surfaces matching their contact proxies;
wood and stone use persistent polygonal material faces. Fine sand uses the
continuous display skin described above, with no permanent cohesive bonds.

Tool heads and bucket interior are closed physical meshes. The spade has a
pointed planform and a concave blade; the axe has a tapered cutting wedge;
the pick has a curved finite point; the bucket has an open mouth, continuous
bottom and end walls. Shafts, grips and bucket bail are display geometry.
Nothing attaches a clod or sand to a tool, and no scoop is spawned or erased.

Growth is a separate state system. Seeds have finite reserves, moisture gates
biological time and growth, reserves convert into biomass, roots extend into
specific material samples, and roots/shoots have mechanical constraints.
Root attachment transfers reaction into soil and can break under load. Each
plant now has three curved, tapered leaves with separate mechanical chains.
The seeded population cap is 32. Shoots
participate in OGC tool contact; buried roots are embedded constraints, not
colliding objects inside their host soil. Root bindings survive refinement.
Biological time runs at 12 hours per simulation second. This first growth law
has no species calibration, full photosynthesis, nutrient transport or life
cycle; water material dynamics are not part of these seven scenes.

Performance implementation
The iterative solver uses the registered OGCConnectionSystem.Respond operation
with borrowed numeric batches. Contract permission, ownership, circuit-breaker,
finite-value validation and invocation tracing remain enforced. Configure and
the legacy Project interface remain available. Unused vertex projection and
JSON array construction/parsing are removed from this response-only path.

BVH nodes exclude disabled self surfaces before descending, and leaves check
individual primitive bounds. Small groups of non-self-colliding surfaces cache
enlarged candidate lists. Every vertex must remain within the cache's movement
envelope; topology/radius changes or excess movement rebuild candidates. Forces,
Hessians and conservative bounds are always recalculated at current positions.
Large contact batches use Apple's worker pool with separate response buffers
and a deterministic reduction. Serial queries remain available for comparison.
Streaming self-contact also uses those workers: creating fragments no longer
switches a material scene back to one worker. Cached and streamed contact are
checked against serial responses in focused native regressions.
Parallel accumulation can change floating-point trajectories; it does not
change material strengths, physical substeps or the contact equations.

The material contact sweep streams pairs from grouped spatial bins. It does not
construct the old quadratic list of mostly distant pairs. It preserves the
previous pair visitation order. The skin retains its last successful sampling spacing, avoiding repeated
over-budget reconstructions; sparse lookup and field evaluation reuse grid samples.

A dynamic radius-search alternative was evaluated and retired in this revision:
it improved the opening but regressed the full 15-second dirt run to 44.6 ms per
physics frame, versus 37.9 ms with grouped bins. Its extra per-particle searches
and grid relocation did not justify replacing the faster retained implementation.
An early convergence exit was also evaluated and removed: essentially every
substep still required all five iterations, while the checks added overhead.

2026-10-29 actual-game mining performance implementation
The default surface activation no longer makes the entire geological column
live on a single shovel stroke. A 0.45 m upper section holds the root mat and
local soil; deeper unstrained material remains implicit. This retires the
whole-column activation alternative in gameplay, not the workshop layered
specimen. Existing regional boundary/coupling approximations remain. The
selected region still uses four 1/240 s substeps and five coupled iterations.

The rejected initial cache design discarded all broad-phase reuse whenever a
surface enabled self-contact. Its replacement caches conservative candidates
per surface pair, including self-contact, under a bounded storage allowance.
Motion and filter/topology changes invalidate relevant caches. Overflow uses
complete streaming; it never silently discards contacts. The retained native
contexts belong to their physical regions rather than being recreated on each
background/foreground swap. Fixed nodes retain supporting contact geometry.

Compact soil uses counted spatial bins, with sparse fallback for dispersed
matter and exact reuse until a sample changes bins. Constraint iteration order
is preserved. Contact multipliers index active samples, so inactive refinement
history does not cause quadratic allocation. Fully fixed shape clusters skip
work whose corrections cannot move any sample.

Numeric registered routes replace per-frame mesh JSON round trips for water
and material presentation. The JSON interfaces remain compatibility adapters.
Accepted Entity material skins are immutable shared buffers. Coarse visibility
only rejects presentation; accepted matter and its collision remain. Unchanged
water triangles retain their spatial memberships. CPU benchmark evidence and
limits live in output/benchmarks/mining-20261029, with the harness in
Host/MiningBenchmark.rs. Existing workshop timing results above remain historical,
not claims about this game benchmark or the current iPhone build.
